A little curiosity goes a long way

The LED won’t light.
What would you check?

The supply is on. The connections and series resistor are correct. The current is zero, and the LED stays dark.

One small change solves “It never lit,” the first Repair Shop puzzle.

Why does its direction matter?

An LED is a diode: it needs the right polarity to light. In this puzzle, reversing the LED restores the intended current path.

On a typical untrimmed through-hole LED, the longer leg is the anode (+); the shorter leg is the cathode (−). Check the component’s markings or datasheet if the legs have been cut.

For a real build, disconnect power before rewiring and keep the correctly sized current-limiting resistor in series. A dark LED can have other causes, too.
