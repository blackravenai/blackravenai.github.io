Common questions

  • Restore a purchase. Open the purchase screen in the app and choose "Restore Purchases". Purchases are tied to your app-store account and restore for free on your devices.
  • What TICKING does, and what it does not decide. You name the article, say whether the material is all new, contains secondhand or is the owner’s own, weigh each filling on your kitchen scale, and name the states you ship to. Ticking resolves your words to the generic terms the rules name, works the percentages by weight, orders them the way each destination’s own text says to, applies that state’s own tolerance, and draws one tag carrying every prescribed element at the largest minimum size any of your destinations sets — with the section that requires each line shown beside it. It does not tell you whether a rule applies to you, whether you need a registry number, or whether you may sell. The percentages are your declaration; Ticking never infers what is inside an article. The state governs; the app does not.
  • Where the rules come from, and how stale they can be. Thirty-one sources, all edicts of government, each read on a stated date and cited section by section — California’s Business and Professions Code and 4 CCR, 34 Pa. Code, Ohio Administrative Code 4101:6, and the bedding statutes and rules of twenty-two further jurisdictions, plus the industry-summary rows named as such. Every tag line, every screen and every export carries the citation and the read date, so you can always see how old the copy is. Rules change between our releases. Verify against the current rule before you order a label run.
  • Why some destinations return no wording. Twenty-six of the fifty-one rows are industry-summary rows: a dated reference records that the jurisdiction has a tagging requirement, but that jurisdiction’s own text was not obtained, so Ticking prescribes no wording for it and puts none on your tag. It says so on the tag screen and on the States tab rather than guessing. Where a jurisdiction’s obtained text covers only some cases — Utah’s and Vermont’s cover all-new articles only, Ohio’s covers new and secondhand but publishes no owner’s-material form — anything outside that scope returns "unavailable" with the reason.
  • Maryland, and why two industry lists disagree. Maryland’s bedding law was repealed in 2008 (2008 Md. Laws ch. 54, effective 1 October 2008), yet free state lists still carry Maryland under both "registration required" and "states without tagging laws". Ticking reports the repeal with the citation and marks the row disputed rather than picking a side.
  • Ambiguous filling names. Many bench words cover more than one regulated term. When a name you type maps to more than one, Ticking marks it AMBIGUOUS and asks you to choose, and a name it does not hold returns NOT A TERM HELD HERE. It never silently picks a term for you, because the term changes the tag.
  • What is free and what is Pro. The whole solve is free for ever, at full accuracy: every element, every destination, the strictest-state intersection, every citation, the true-scale tag against the inch rule, all 51 jurisdictions with their sources and dates, the Prove-it screen, three articles in the tag book, and export of everything you entered — the printer-ready tag text and the whole book as JSON — free for ever, including after a plan ends. Pro never buys a more accurate tag. Pro adds an unlimited tag book, dated version history on every re-solve, identity presets, and the run sheet — the whole book as one printer-ready document.
  • Subscription terms. TICKING Pro is $29.99 a year and opens with a 21-day free trial (annual plan only, offered once per store account), or $5.99 a month with no trial, or $79.99 once for lifetime. The yearly and monthly plans renew automatically at the then-current price for the same period unless renewal is turned off at least 24 hours before the period ends; the lifetime purchase is a single payment, never renews, and there is nothing to cancel. Cancel a renewing plan any time in your store account — on iOS, Settings › your name › Subscriptions; on Android, the Play Store › Payments and subscriptions. Cancelling stops the next charge and the period you have paid for runs to its end. We cannot cancel it for you.
  • My Pro plan lapsed. Nothing of yours is deleted or hidden. Every article already in the book stays readable, editable, deletable and exportable in full — including the ones saved past the free three, and including the citations they were solved with. A lapse blocks only saving a new article above three; it never takes an article away.
  • Prove it. Open the Prove-it screen and Ticking re-derives ten label forms and worked examples the regulators themselves published — California’s official forms and its worked plumage examples among them — on your own handset, against the same engine, showing the published wording beside the wording Ticking produced. Nothing is fetched and nothing is cached. The full development harness runs 18 published specimens and 1,920 enumerated cases outside the app.
  • Does it need a signal? No. Every resolution, every percentage and every citation lookup is arithmetic over bundled tables, so the app opens, solves, records and exports with the network switched off — which is also why nothing about your articles ever leaves the handset.
  • Reset or delete my data. Your work is stored on your device. Clearing the app's data or uninstalling the app removes it. There is no server-side account to delete.
  • Report a bug or request a feature. Email us with what you expected and what happened — screenshots help a lot.
