Common questions

  • Restore a purchase. Open the purchase screen in the app and choose "Restore Purchases". Purchases are tied to your app-store account and restore for free on your devices.
  • What WINNOW does, and what it does not decide. WINNOW derives the label element set the Federal Seed Act regulations and the seed law of the state you select ask for, counts the months to the day that lot’s germination test runs out on each state’s own statutory basis, and writes packet text from the figures you entered. It never estimates or infers a germination percentage — that number is your laboratory’s result, carried, never invented. It does not decide whether you may ship seed into any state, whether you need a labeler registration, or whether your seed belongs to a certified class. It is not legal advice. Every element names the section that requires it, quotes it, and shows the date the text was read. You own the label that goes on the packet — check it against your state before the run prints.
  • Where the rules come from, and how current they are. Two bodies of rule text are transcribed. The federal set is 7 CFR part 201 — the labelling sections and the 68-kind germination standards table of § 201.31 — from the eCFR. The state set is 27 states’ seed labelling and germination-validity provisions, each row carrying its citation, its source page, the date it was fetched and the state’s own quoted sentence. Every state row on screen carries the line “This can be stale. Check your state before you print.” Rules change; a date on the screen is the honest way to say so.
  • Which states does it compute? Twenty-seven. The other twenty-three and the District of Columbia are named on screen as not read, and they compute nothing rather than guessing. Four of the twenty-seven rows carry no validity period and thirteen carry no packet element list; those are shown under “what WINNOW will not guess” rather than filled in with a default. Where a state measures from the test itself and your lot records only a test month, WINNOW takes the first day of that month — the conservative end — and says so on the screen.
  • What is free and what is Pro. The instrument is free forever: any one destination solved in full — and the solve moves across your whole list — with every element, every citation, every quoted sentence and the month arithmetic; the federal § 201.29 conditional germination panel; the stamped expiry block; copyable packet text; three lots on the rack; the alarm for a test that has already run out; the cited rules table; the whole “Prove it” screen; and export of everything you entered as JSON, CSV and text. Pro adds every destination solved at once and ordered by which closes first, unlimited lots on the rack, the 60-day watch naming the state that closes first, editable state rows with the shipped row kept alongside, and the lot book of dated solve records. Pro never buys a more accurate answer — the solver is the same solver on both tiers, and your own data is never gated.
  • Subscription terms. Winnow Pro is $24.99 a year and opens with a 21-day free introductory offer (annual plan only, offered once per store account), or $4.99 a month with no trial, or $69.99 once for lifetime. The yearly and monthly plans renew automatically at the then-current price for the same period unless renewal is turned off at least 24 hours before the period ends; the lifetime purchase is a single payment, never renews, and there is nothing to cancel. Cancel a renewing plan any time in your store account — on iOS, Settings › your name › Subscriptions; on Android, the Play Store › Payments and subscriptions. Cancelling stops the next charge and the period you have paid for runs to its end. We cannot cancel it for you, and the app will not ask you to stay.
  • My Pro plan lapsed. Nothing of yours is deleted, hidden or truncated. Every lot on the rack, every dated solve record, every destination list, your labeler line and your own state edits stay readable, editable, deletable and exportable, however many lots there are. What stops is solving every destination at once, holding more than three lots, the 60-day watch and editing a state row. A lapse lowers a ceiling on new work; it never takes a record away.
  • Prove it. Open the “Prove it” screen and WINNOW re-runs a set of the answer-key cases on your own handset and shows its answers beside the expected ones, so you can check the instrument rather than trust it.
  • Does it need a signal in the barn? No. The rule tables are bundled with the app and the solve is arithmetic, so it opens, solves, stamps and exports with the network switched off.
  • Reset or delete my data. Your work is stored on your device. Clearing the app's data or uninstalling the app removes it. There is no server-side account to delete.
  • Report a bug or request a feature. Email us with what you expected and what happened — screenshots help a lot.

Sources & attribution

WINNOW’s rule tables are transcribed from primary sources and cited line by line in the app. The federal tables come from 7 CFR part 201, including the § 201.31 germination standards table for 68 kinds, taken from the eCFR — a work of the United States Government in the public domain under 17 U.S.C. § 105. The state tables are 27 states’ seed labelling and germination-validity provisions, each transcribed from that state’s own published text with its citation, its source page, its fetch date and a verbatim quoted sentence; the builder fails if a quoted anchor is not present verbatim in the fetched page, which is what makes “no uncited rows” checkable. The engine was written against an answer key and a null baseline both recorded before any solver code existed: it reproduces 8 of 8 published worked examples, agrees with an independent derivation on 1,356 of 1,356 enumerated cases, passes 5 of 5 property tests, and traces 27 of 27 state rows to a cited, dated and quoted source. The null baseline — reprinting last year’s packet — scores 1 of 8 worked examples and 0 of 1,356 cases, and 97.2% of its reprinted packet-destination pairs are non-conforming, 85.2% of them carrying an expired test and 55.5% omitting a required element.
