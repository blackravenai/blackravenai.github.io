Common questions

  • Restore a purchase. Open the purchase screen in the app and choose "Restore Purchases". Purchases are tied to your app-store account and restore for free on your devices.
  • What is free and what is Pro. One product, complete and forever: the principal display panel measured from your jar, the panel format the rules permit at that size, the exemption check, every declaration with its percent daily value, the ingredient statement, the allergen review with the phrase that triggered each allergen, the whole set drawn at true 1:1, its SVG and its print sheet, and JSON and CSV export of everything you have entered. Pro adds an unlimited product library, duplicating a product into a new jar size or a reformulation with its revision history, and the library print sheet with every label at 1:1 on one sheet. Pro never buys a better answer.
  • Subscription terms. Pro is $24.99 a year and opens with a 21-day free trial (annual plan only, offered once per account), or $4.99 a month with no trial, or $74.99 once for lifetime. The yearly and monthly plans renew automatically at the then-current price for the same period unless renewal is turned off at least 24 hours before the period ends; the lifetime purchase is a single payment, never renews, and there is nothing to cancel. Cancel a renewing plan any time in your store account — on iOS, Settings › your name › Subscriptions; on Android, the Play Store › Payments and subscriptions. Cancelling stops the next charge and the period you have paid for runs to its end.
  • My Pro plan lapsed. Nothing of yours is deleted or hidden. Every product, every ingredient row, every revision and every label stays on the device, stays readable, stays editable and stays exportable, including the 1:1 SVG. Exactly one capability stops: adding the next product. A lapse lowers a ceiling; it never takes a record away.
  • LABELWRIGHT does not approve your label. It draws the mandatory particulars in the format of 21 CFR part 101 from what you enter and cites the section behind each one. It does not review, certify or approve anything, it does not tell you whether you qualify for any exemption, and it makes no claim that a label it draws satisfies any authority. You are responsible for the label you print, and the app says so on its main surface and in About.
  • Why it will not write “may contain”. An advisory cross-contact statement is a claim about your kitchen, not a function of your ingredient list, so the app refuses to write one and tells you why. It derives the FALCPA “Contains” statement from the ingredients you typed, names the phrase that triggered each allergen so you can check it, lets you strike any of them, and lets you add one the ingredient text cannot show — a compound ingredient or a supplier specification — marked as added by you.
  • Where the numbers come from. Ingredient composition is matched against a copy of the USDA FoodData Central public-domain data shipped inside the app; the rounding rules, increment bands, reference amounts, type heights and panel formats are implemented from 21 CFR part 101 and cited section by section on screen. The percent daily value is computed on the declared (rounded) figure, which 21 CFR 101.9(d)(7)(ii) permits, so the panel reproduces itself from its own printed numbers. The app says which basis it used.
  • The state cottage-food wording is a template, not advice. The disclaimers are each state’s own published wording, reproduced for eight states with a custom field for the rest. LABELWRIGHT never decides which law applies to you or whether you qualify under it.
  • Weigh the finished batch. The panel depends on what came off the scale after cooking, not on what went in. Enter the measured finished weight and the app computes the loss for you rather than modelling it.
  • Restore purchases. Open More › Settings and tap Restore purchases; entitlements are re-checked with the store.
  • Reset or delete my data. Your work is stored on your device. Clearing the app's data or uninstalling the app removes it. There is no server-side account to delete.
  • Report a bug or request a feature. Email us with what you expected and what happened — screenshots help a lot.

Sources & attribution

LABELWRIGHT’s declarations, rounding rules, increment bands, percent daily values, reference amounts customarily consumed, principal-display-panel geometry, minimum type heights, panel-format eligibility and small-package exemptions are implemented from 21 CFR part 101 (a public-domain work of the United States Government) and cited section by section in the app and in its design record. Allergen derivation follows FALCPA as codified at 21 U.S.C. 343(w), including the refined-oil exemption at 343(w)(2), with 21 CFR 137.105 governing when a bare “flour” means wheat. Ingredient composition comes from the USDA FoodData Central public-domain dataset, a subset of which ships inside the app. Every figure in the app’s published answer key was measured against real labels, and the one key that misses its threshold is reported as a miss rather than tuned to pass.
