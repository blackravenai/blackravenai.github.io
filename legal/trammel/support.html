Common questions

  • Restore a purchase. Open the purchase screen in the app and choose "Restore Purchases". Purchases are tied to your app-store account and restore for free on your devices.
  • What is free and what is Pro. The whole instrument is free forever: the complete tangent-system solve, every figure it produces, the face mould for the upper face of the plank printed 1:1 across as many sheets as it takes, the Prove-it check run on your own handset, millimetres or inches and sixteenths, three turns in the stair book, and export of everything in it as JSON or CSV. Pro adds the lower-face mould, the falling-line strip, the written cut sheet copied out as text, and an unlimited stair book. Pro never buys a better number — the solver is the same solver on both sides.
  • Subscription terms. Pro is $39.99 a year and opens with a 21-day free trial (yearly plan only, offered once per account), or $6.99 a month with no trial, or $119.99 once for lifetime. The yearly and monthly plans renew automatically at the then-current price for the same period unless renewal is turned off at least 24 hours before the period ends; the lifetime purchase is a single payment, never renews, and there is nothing to cancel. Cancel a renewing plan any time in your store account — on iOS, Settings › your name › Subscriptions; on Android, the Play Store › Payments and subscriptions. Cancelling stops the next charge and the period you have paid for runs to its end.
  • My Pro plan lapsed. Nothing of yours is deleted or hidden. Every turn in the stair book still opens, still shows its full figures, still prints its upper-face mould at 1:1, still edits, still deletes and still exports as JSON or CSV, including books far longer than the free tier would let you build today. What stops is striking a fourth new turn, the lower face, the falling line and the written cut sheet. A lapse lowers a ceiling on new work; it never takes a record away.
  • Where the figures come from. TRAMMEL solves the tangent system as three-dimensional vectors rather than reading a table. From the plan angle of the tangents, the pitch of each flight, the well radius and the section of your rail it derives the pitch of the plank, the developed angle of the tangents on the mould, the semi-axes of the mould ellipse, the twist bevel at each joint, the mould width at each spring, the plank thickness and the block the wreath must be cut from.
  • Prove it. The app carries ten shipped turns and re-solves them on your handset, then reconstructs the wreath in three dimensions from the sheet it just printed and re-measures it — centre line, joints out of square, finished rise. Open the Prove-it screen and check the instrument rather than trust it.
  • Printing at 1:1. The sheet carries a 100 mm check bar. Print at 100% scale with page fitting off, then measure the bar on any sheet before you cut a block from the mould.
  • Set the pitch from the steps. If you know the riser and the going rather than the pitch in degrees, type them on the rod and TRAMMEL strikes the pitch from them. It outputs no step, stringer or flight figure — it is an input affordance on the pitch instruments.
  • What it will not do. TRAMMEL sets out geometry. It makes no structural statement and no building-code statement about rise, going, headroom, guard height or baluster spacing, and it does not check your stair against any standard. Check the mould against the rod and the block before the bandsaw touches the plank.
  • Reset or delete my data. Your work is stored on your device. Clearing the app's data or uninstalling the app removes it. There is no server-side account to delete.
  • Report a bug or request a feature. Email us with what you expected and what happened — screenshots help a lot.

Sources & attribution

TRAMMEL’s tangent-system solve is checked before it ships against an independently produced answer key of 1,818 mapped values, reproducing 1,799 of them (98.95%) against 718 (39.49%) for an ablated null solver; every one of the 19 misses is accounted for, 8 lying outside the frozen pitch band and 11 demonstrated self-contradictions in the key itself. Over 300 frozen turns the wreath is reconstructed in three dimensions from the app’s own printed sheet and re-measured: 0 failing turns against 300 of 300 for the null solver, with a worst centre-line error of 5.7e-13 mm, joints out of square by at most 1.7e-6°, and no turn whose plank is too thin to contain the rail. The method, its thresholds, its ablations and every constant are cited line by line in the app’s design record and its answer key. The historical rule quoted in the app is from George Collings, A Practical Treatise on Handrailing, 1890 (public domain).
