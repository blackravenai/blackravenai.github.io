Common questions

  • Restore a purchase. Open the purchase screen in the app and choose "Restore Purchases". Purchases are tied to your app-store account and restore for free on your devices.
  • What GLUEFLAP does, and what it does not decide. You open the template PDF you are about to list. GLUEFLAP reads the sheet, then runs five checks over what it recovered: whether every numbered edge has the partner it needs, whether the piece index is continuous and every indexed piece is actually on a page, whether every page reference resolves, whether the assembly order resolves, and whether the scale block agrees with the sheet it is printed on. Each break is reported with the arithmetic behind it and ringed on a to-scale miniature of your own page, at its own coordinate. It does not unfold models, it does not generate templates, it does not renumber anything and it never edits your file. It does not tell you a template is error-free, and it makes no claim that a model will assemble, fit or hold. The decision to publish stays yours.
  • UNCHECKED is not a pass, and neither is silence. Where a page is a flattened raster, or where no reading of the sheet can decide which edge a number names, GLUEFLAP counts the check it declined and prints that count on the face of the report rather than assuming the page was fine. A file it could read nothing from shows a dash, never a green nought: a document with no pages raises a named error, and an all-raster sheet or a sheet with no recoverable pieces returns “not checked” with the reason.
  • The finished height is not checked. Recovering a model’s three-dimensional height from a flat net requires the fold to be solved, which GLUEFLAP does not do. The report states the height as declared, not checked, and says so on the page.
  • The length check is a per-piece check. A single mating pair is called out only at a gross difference; systematic resizing is caught across a piece’s mated edges. One edge redrawn on an otherwise-correct piece, below that threshold, will not be flagged — the app is explicit about this rather than implying that every geometric error is caught.
  • What it cannot read. A template whose numbers were converted to outlines before export carries no text to recover, and is reported as unchecked with a count rather than passed. Pepakura’s own .pdo project format is not read at all: no public description of it exists. PDF is the input.
  • What is free and what is Pro. The audit is free for ever: any template, as often as you like, all five checks, the full working behind every break, the UNCHECKED count, the proof sheet on screen, three templates on the shelf with every revision of them kept and readable, and all three exports — JSON, CSV and the printable correction sheet — never gated, now or ever. Pro adds a shelf with no limit, the re-audit diff, the printed proof sheet and the catalogue pass. Pro never buys a more accurate number — every check, the mating tolerance and every piece of working are identical on both tiers.
  • Subscription terms. GLUEFLAP Pro is $14.99 a year and opens with a 21-day free introductory offer (annual plan only, offered once per store account), or $3.49 a month with no trial, or $39.99 once for lifetime. The yearly and monthly plans renew automatically at the then-current price for the same period unless renewal is turned off at least 24 hours before the period ends; the lifetime purchase is a single payment, never renews, and there is nothing to cancel. Cancel a renewing plan any time in your store account — on iOS, Settings › your name › Subscriptions; on Android, the Play Store › Payments and subscriptions. Cancelling stops the next charge and the period you have paid for runs to its end. We cannot cancel it for you, and the app will not ask you to stay.
  • My Pro plan lapsed. Nothing of yours is deleted, hidden or truncated. Every template on the shelf stays readable and stays exportable, however many there are — a shelf built to ten stays at ten. What stops is filing a new fourth template, opening the re-audit diff, printing the proof sheet and running a catalogue pass. A fourth template still audits in full and still exports; it is simply not filed. A lapse lowers a ceiling on new work; it never takes a record away.
  • Prove it. Open the Prove-it screen and GLUEFLAP re-runs ten templates whose answers are known on your own handset, showing its answers beside the expected ones, so you can check the instrument rather than trust it.
  • Does it need a signal? No. Every parse, measurement and check is arithmetic on your own document, so the app opens, reads, audits and exports with the network switched off — which is also why an unlisted template never leaves the machine.
  • Reset or delete my data. Your work is stored on your device. Clearing the app's data or uninstalling the app removes it. There is no server-side account to delete.
  • Report a bug or request a feature. Email us with what you expected and what happened — screenshots help a lot.

Sources & attribution

GLUEFLAP’s engine was measured against an answer key frozen before any engine code existed, and the numbers are published rather than summarised. The key holds 904 authored template PDFs — 320 consistent base sheets, 540 mutations across ten known defect types, and 44 clean templates carrying legitimate open backs, documented spares and declared repeats — with pieces ranging from 10 to 394 per template. No third-party template file is used, reproduced or bundled anywhere in the app or its harness. On the last measured run the engine met every ranked threshold: 99.8% overall recall (539 of 540), with the worst single defect type at 98.2%; 1.10 false flags per 100 consistent templates, none at all over the 44 clean ones; and 100.0% page-read coverage over 404,154 numbered marks, with the unreadable raster pages named exactly right in all 34 cases that carried one. A null baseline recorded first — a page-count check — scores 0.0% recall on all ten defect types and 0.0% coverage. Three limits are published rather than hidden. The single miss is named on the record: on one case both halves of a mismatched edge sit on pieces small enough that no reading of the sheet decides which edge each number names, and it is reported as unchecked rather than guessed. The corpus is synthetic by construction, deliberately randomising compression, fonts, placement operators, label formats and document order so the reader cannot be tuned to one dialect — but it remains a corpus this build authored, and the app says so. And no claim of recall against real published papercraft corrections is made anywhere, because fewer than fifteen such corrections were obtainable; that absence is printed on every report rather than papered over.
