Common questions

  • Restore a purchase. Open the purchase screen in the app and choose "Restore Purchases". Purchases are tied to your app-store account and restore for free on your devices.
  • What CAPPINGS does, and does not do. It reads the rules you and it have transcribed and works out, for the jar in your hand, which identity statement the recipe permits, the net quantity in both units, the elements the jurisdiction you sell in requires, the smallest cap height the panel area allows, and where on the panel each element sits. It is not a regulator, it does not review or clear anything, it does not advise you on the law, and it submits nothing to anyone. Your state department of agriculture has the final word on your jar.
  • Which jurisdictions carry a state rule. Twenty-five, each transcribed from that state’s own published text with its citation and the date it was read. For every other state CAPPINGS solves the federal set only and says so on the bench rather than pretending to a rule it has not read. Every row is editable, and an edited row is marked as yours and drives the solve from then on.
  • Why a required sentence appears or disappears. Several states condition a statement on a fact about your operation — whether the honey was bottled on premises the state has looked at, whether you sell only direct to the consumer, how much you sell in a year. Change that fact in Settings and every element that depends on it re-solves, and the batch book shows you which of your past lots would now be labelled differently.
  • The infant statement. Where it appears it is quoted as a named jurisdiction’s own requirement or recommendation, with its citation and the date read. It is never the app’s own advice, and where a state says expressly that it does not require it, CAPPINGS does not add it.
  • What is free and what is Pro. Solving a jar is free forever and nothing about it is cut down: every element, every citation, every refusal with the rule behind it, the whole arithmetic, THE PANEL on screen at true scale, the citation sheet, the fully editable rule table, the Prove-it screen, and the batch book up to three lots. Pro adds print-ready label artwork as SVG at true inch scale, the batch book past three lots, the re-solve diff across every lot you have bottled, and all twenty-five jurisdictions solved at once. Pro never buys a different answer — the engine is identical on both tiers, and your own data is never gated.
  • Subscription terms. CAPPINGS Pro is .99 a year and opens with a 30-day free trial (annual plan only, offered once per store account), or .99 a month with no trial, or .99 once for lifetime. The yearly and monthly plans renew automatically at the then-current price for the same period unless renewal is turned off at least 24 hours before the period ends; the lifetime purchase is a single payment, never renews, and there is nothing to cancel. Cancel a renewing plan any time in your store account — on iOS, Settings › your name › Subscriptions; on Android, the Play Store › Payments and subscriptions. Cancelling stops the next charge and the period you have paid for runs to its end.
  • Why the trial is thirty days. A custom label print run takes two to three weeks from order to delivery. A shorter trial would charge you before the labels the app exists to produce are in your hands.
  • My Pro plan lapsed. Nothing of yours is deleted or hidden. Every lot in the batch book stays readable, stays exportable as CSV and JSON, and stays deletable — including books far longer than the free tier would let you build today. What stops is artwork export, committing a fourth lot, the re-solve diff and the all-states sweep. A lapse lowers a ceiling on new work; it never takes a record away.
  • Prove it. The app carries the regulator’s own published worked examples on the handset. Open Prove it and CAPPINGS re-solves all of them in front of you and shows its answers against the known ones, so you can check the instrument rather than trust it.
  • Printing at true scale. The SVG artwork is written at the panel dimensions you entered, in real inches. Print at 100% scale with page fitting off, and measure the printed panel with a rule before it goes to a label printer as artwork.
  • Reset or delete my data. Your work is stored on your device. Clearing the app's data or uninstalling the app removes it. There is no server-side account to delete.
  • Report a bug or request a feature. Email us with what you expected and what happened — screenshots help a lot.

Sources & attribution

CAPPINGS’ federal arithmetic is implemented from the Fair Packaging and Labeling Act and Title 21 of the Code of Federal Regulations — principally 21 CFR 101.1 (principal display panel area), 101.3 (identity statement), 101.4 (ingredient statement), 101.7 (net quantity of contents), together with FDA’s published honey labelling guidance — all works of the United States Government in the public domain under 17 U.S.C. § 105. The twenty-five state rows are transcribed from each state’s own published statute, administrative code or department guidance, and each carries its citation and the date it was read. The engine is measured against an answer key and a null baseline both recorded before any solver code existed: it reproduces 18 of 18 FDA worked examples, agrees with an independent derivation on 26,208 of 26,208 enumerated cases with 0 property violations, traces 25 of 25 state rows to a cited and dated source, and passes 31 of 31 net-weight and type-size cases. The null baseline — reprinting last year’s label — scores 1 of 18 and 0 of 26,208, and across the grid 19.7% of its labels carry a state statement that was never printed, 40.0% carry a claim with no recorded basis and 31.7% omit an element the state requires. Every threshold, every deviation and every constant is cited line by line in the app’s design record and its answer key.
