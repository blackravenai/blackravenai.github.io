Common questions

  • Restore a purchase. Open the purchase screen in the app and choose "Restore Purchases". Purchases are tied to your app-store account and restore for free on your devices.
  • What is free and what is Pro. One complete roof at a time is free forever, with nothing about it cut down: the diminishing courses solved off your own crate, the landing solve, the bond under your side-lap floor, up to two slopes so a hip and the slope it closes against can both be set out, the hip and valley cut list, the rod, the setting-out sheet, the diagnostics, the Prove-it screen, both unit systems and every export. Pro adds the roof book past the first roof, the yard of leftover slate laid across roofs into the slope you have open, as many slopes on a roof as the roof has, and printing and sharing the sheet and the cut list. Pro never buys a better number — the solver is the same solver on both sides.
  • Subscription terms. Pro is $44.99 a year and opens with a 21-day free trial (annual plan only, offered once per account), or $6.99 a month with no trial, or $119.99 once for lifetime. The yearly and monthly plans renew automatically at the then-current price for the same period unless renewal is turned off at least 24 hours before the period ends; the lifetime purchase is a single payment, never renews, and there is nothing to cancel. Cancel a renewing plan any time in your store account — on iOS, Settings › your name › Subscriptions; on Android, the Play Store › Payments and subscriptions. Cancelling stops the next charge and the period you have paid for runs to its end.
  • My Pro plan lapsed. Nothing of yours is deleted or hidden. Every roof in the book, every slope on it and every row on the yard stays on the device, stays readable, stays editable, stays exportable and stays deletable, including roofs with more slopes than the free tier would let you create today. A lapse lowers a ceiling on new work; it never takes a record away.
  • Where the numbers come from. GAUGEROD solves the actual gauge course by course rather than reading one minimum gauge off a table. It takes the slate lengths you have on the ground, your headlap and your minimum side lap, and lays the courses out so the head lap holds everywhere, the margins never invert, the top course is never a sliver, the bond clears the side lap over your real crate of random widths, and the courses close through a hip or a valley. The cut list gives the tail, head and off-the-corner figure for every arris piece with its bevel angle.
  • Prove it. The app carries 24 public-domain test cases — US Department of Commerce Simplified Practice Recommendation No. 14 (1924) Table 1 and the National Slate Association’s Slate Roofs (1926) worked exposures. Open the Prove-it screen and GAUGEROD re-solves them on your handset and shows its answer against the published one, so you can check the instrument rather than trust it.
  • Two things it will tell you it cannot do. A crate whose widest slate is not much more than twice your side lap cannot hold a random-width bond, so GAUGEROD squares the slope to one width and says so rather than drawing a bond that will not stand. Two slopes of different pitch cannot run their courses through the arris between them, so it cuts both sides to the line and says so. Both refusals are checked by the app’s own test harness — it cannot dodge a check by claiming one.
  • Slate and stone slate only. GAUGEROD sets out slate and stone slate. It does not set out plain tile, and it says so on its own surfaces rather than quietly producing a number for it.
  • Headlap is yours to set. GAUGEROD ships no standard’s minimum headlap and quotes no paywalled standard. You enter the headlap and the minimum side lap you are working to and cite your own source for them; the app records the source alongside the roof and prints it on the setting-out sheet. What the specification for your roof requires remains a matter for you, the specifier and the slate supplier.
  • Measure the roof and the crate yourself. GAUGEROD sets out from the figures you type. It does not measure your rafters, sort your slate or check your battens. Check the rod against the roof before the first batten goes on.
  • Restore purchases. Open More and tap Restore purchases; entitlements are re-checked with the store.
  • Reset or delete my data. Your work is stored on your device. Clearing the app's data or uninstalling the app removes it. There is no server-side account to delete.
  • Report a bug or request a feature. Email us with what you expected and what happened — screenshots help a lot.

Sources & attribution

GAUGEROD’s coursing solve is checked against a published answer key before it ships: 34 of 34 diminishing-course gauges reproduced exactly from Hill, Traditional Roofing No. 5 (2006); 30 of 30 exposures within one slate of US Department of Commerce Simplified Practice Recommendation No. 14 (1924) Table 1, 23 of them exact; 15 of 15 worked values reproduced exactly from Jenkins, Traditional Roofing No. 6 (2007) and the National Slate Association’s Slate Roofs (1926); zero violations of eight geometric invariants over 500 frozen test roofs, against 4,035 violations for the naive rule the same harness scores; and zero batten positions more than one millimetre out when the app’s own exported sheet is re-parsed by an independent checker. The 1924 and 1926 sources are public domain and are the only cases shipped inside the app. The method, its thresholds and every constant are cited line by line in the app’s design record and its answer key.
