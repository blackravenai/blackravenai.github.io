Common questions

  • Restore a purchase. Open the purchase screen in the app and choose "Restore Purchases". Purchases are tied to your app-store account and restore for free on your devices.
  • What is free and what is Pro. The full reconcile is free forever, on a release of any size, with unlimited runs, every finding printed with its working, the tally rail, the UNCHECKED count, the self-test screen and all four exports — report, findings CSV, full reconcile CSV and shelf JSON. Free keeps one release on the shelf. Pro adds the unlimited shelf, every version of a release with the re-audit diff, and house defaults (declared spares and substitutions). Pro never buys a better answer: the engine is identical on both tiers.
  • Subscription terms. STUDCOUNT Pro is $39.99 a year and opens with a 21-day free trial (yearly plan only, offered once per store account), or $6.99 a month with no trial, or $99.99 once for lifetime. The yearly and monthly plans renew automatically at the then-current price for the same period unless renewal is turned off at least 24 hours before the period ends; the lifetime purchase is a single payment, never renews, and there is nothing to cancel. Cancel a renewing plan any time in your store account — on iOS, Settings › your name › Subscriptions; on Android, the Play Store › Payments and subscriptions. Cancelling stops the next charge and the period you have paid for runs to its end.
  • My Pro plan lapsed. Nothing of yours is deleted or locked. Every release you kept stays readable, stays exportable and stays deletable, including shelves longer than the free tier would let you build today. A lapse lowers a ceiling on new records; it never takes a record away.
  • Which files STUDCOUNT reads. Models: LDraw .ldr, .mpd and .dat. Lists: a Rebrickable inventory CSV, a generic CSV with a part, colour and quantity column under common header names, and a BrickLink wanted-list XML. Studio .io and LDD .lxf/.lxfml are declined on purpose — neither container has a published description, and a parser we cannot verify is worse than an honest refusal. Export LDraw from your design tool and audit that.
  • Why some rows say UNCHECKED. A BrickLink wanted-list XML carries BrickLink colour ids, and we do not bundle that colour table because it has no licence we can verify. Those rows are reconciled on part id and quantity only and every one of them is marked UNCHECKED on the report’s face rather than guessed. The same applies to an LDraw colour code with no unambiguous catalogue equivalent.
  • Prove it. The app carries a self-test you can run on your own handset: it re-runs shipped synthetic releases whose defects are known, with the same matcher the development harness uses, and shows you what it found and what it flagged wrongly — so you can check the instrument rather than trust it.
  • What STUDCOUNT does, and does not do. It compares the model your instructions were generated from against the parts list you are about to publish, and prints every disagreement with its working. It does not judge whether a model is buildable, stable or safe, it does not build instructions, and it makes no claim that a release is error-free. The last read of a release is yours.
  • Brick-compatible. STUDCOUNT is an independent tool. It is not affiliated with, endorsed by, sponsored by or approved by the LEGO Group, BrickLink, Rebrickable or LDraw.org, and it bundles no third-party instruction set, parts list or part imagery.
  • Reset or delete my data. Your work is stored on your device. Clearing the app's data or uninstalling the app removes it. There is no server-side account to delete.
  • Report a bug or request a feature. Email us with what you expected and what happened — screenshots help a lot.

Sources & attribution

STUDCOUNT parses the LDraw file format — line types, 0 FILE and 0 STEP metas, sub-file references with instance multipliers, colour-16 inheritance and cycle-guarded expansion — to the published LDraw.org File Format specification. Its bundled catalogue of parts, colours and part-colour combinations is compiled from the Rebrickable catalogue downloads, which their own page releases for any purpose, and its LDraw colour codes come from the LDraw.org Parts Library LDConfig.ldr under CC BY 2.0 and CC BY 4.0. Every source, licence quotation and threshold is cited line by line in the app’s design record and answer key. BrickLink’s colour table is deliberately not used.
