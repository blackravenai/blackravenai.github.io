Common questions

  • Restore a purchase. Open the purchase screen in the app and choose "Restore Purchases". Purchases are tied to your app-store account and restore for free on your devices.
  • What is free and what is Pro. The instrument is free forever: unlimited reads on the live camera, the full dome and the verdict score, the top-ranked finding with its cause and its sized correction, one saved set with its whole read history, the HOW IT READS reference with every citation, and complete JSON and CSV export. Pro adds every finding rather than the first, unlimited sets, THE LADDER and DRIFT, all five looks, reading a still from your photo library, and the before/after card. Pro never buys a better measurement — the same engine runs on both tiers; Pro only changes how much of the reading the screen posts.
  • Subscription terms. Pro is $19.99 a year and opens with a 21-day free trial (yearly plan only, offered once per store account), or $3.99 a month with no trial, or $39.99 once for lifetime. The yearly and monthly plans renew automatically at the then-current price for the same period unless renewal is turned off at least 24 hours before the period ends; the lifetime purchase is a single payment, never renews, and there is nothing to cancel. Cancel a renewing plan any time in your store account — on iOS, Settings › your name › Subscriptions; on Android, the Play Store › Payments and subscriptions. Cancelling stops the next charge and the period you have paid for runs to its end.
  • My Pro plan lapsed. Nothing of yours is deleted or hidden. Every read stays listed with its own numbers, stays readable, stays editable, stays deletable, and both exports stay complete — including reads in sets past the free limit. A lapse lowers a ceiling on new records; it never takes a record away.
  • Does it upload my face? No. There is no upload path in the app at all. The frame is measured in memory and thrown away, and the app works in aeroplane mode. The camera permission is asked for only when you tap the camera, and if you refuse it you can still read a still picture you choose from your library.
  • It could not find my face. The subject region is fitted from skin chroma. A deeply gelled key, a face very far from the camera or no face in frame makes the fit fail, and FALLOFF says so in plain words and places a default centre region rather than pretending. Drag the region onto your face and read again, or step closer.
  • Why are the corrections in centimetres? Because “move your fill closer” is not an instruction. FALLOFF converts a measured stop difference into a distance using the inverse-square law and the key and fill distances you entered in Settings, which default to 1.50 m and 1.80 m. The footer of every read states the assumption it used; set your real distances and the numbers get better.
  • Why does it sometimes refuse a Kelvin figure? Because a face whose lit cheek is at or near clipping carries no usable colour information. Rather than print a temperature it cannot defend, FALLOFF withholds it and raises the clipping finding instead — which is the thing to fix before the colour figure would be worth reading anyway.
  • What FALLOFF does, and does not do. It measures the light and framing in the frame you point it at and tells you what to move and by how much. It does not retouch, filter, relight or record video, it does not judge your content, and it makes no claim about how any audience will respond. The room is the authority; the app reports what it measured of it.
  • Reset or delete my data. Your work is stored on your device. Clearing the app's data or uninstalling the app removes it. There is no server-side account to delete.
  • Report a bug or request a feature. Email us with what you expected and what happened — screenshots help a lot.

Sources & attribution

FALLOFF’s measurements are computed from published colour science and photographic practice, cited line by line in the app’s design record: sRGB is linearised to CIE XYZ under IEC 61966-2-1, lightness is CIE L*, correlated colour temperature and the green/magenta Duv term follow McCamy’s cubic approximation on CIE 1960 uv, and key-to-fill separation is reported in photographic stops as log2 of the ratio of trimmed linear-luminance medians. Accuracy is measured, not asserted: 160 synthetic scenes whose lighting ratio, illuminant mix, skin exposure, background separation and framing are known by construction are re-measured from pixels alone on every release, and each figure’s published error budget is set on the 90th percentile of that run.
