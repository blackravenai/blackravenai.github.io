Common questions

  • Restore a purchase. Open the purchase screen in the app and choose "Restore Purchases". Purchases are tied to your app-store account and restore for free on your devices.
  • What CLEANPLATE measures, and what it does not decide. CLEANPLATE measures one frame of your own lit backing with the subject standing in it: how far the backing’s luminance ranges across a fixed six-by-four grid, in stops, with the dark region named; how much green from the backing is landing on the crown, the face edge and the shoulder; the backing’s hue and chroma; how the backing sits against the subject in stops and in metres; and how much of the backing is in the subject’s own shadow. It then names the light to move. It does not key, replace, grade or retouch anything, it does not judge whether your setup is right, and it makes no claim about how any editor, keyer or platform will treat your footage. Where a frame is not good enough to measure, it refuses it by name and gives you no number at all — that refusal is the honest answer, not a failure.
  • Where do the ranges come from? Every figure is shown against a convention you pick and can edit, and each convention carries its source and its date on the screen. They are conventions, not rules, and they are not ours. If your house spec differs, enter it and the app judges against yours.
  • What is the reference frame for? Spill is the one quantity a single frame cannot see directly, because the app has no way of knowing what the subject looks like with no green landing on them. Take a second frame on the same tripod with the backing lights off and it can simply subtract. With one frame the three indices are marked inferred; with two they are marked measured, and the CSV carries a column so a reader can always tell which they are looking at. The app’s own measured figures for both paths are printed in its “Prove it” and “How it works” screens.
  • What is free and what is Pro. The instrument is free forever: the complete read — every measured number, the full evenness map, the spill ticks, the corrections and the reason behind each one — on one stand and three saved reads, with export. Pro adds unlimited stands and unlimited reads, re-set (a saved read replayed as target bands behind the live map so you rig to the same numbers on the second visit), drift (a stand’s reads over time, so a light that has crept down shows up), your own custom conventions, and batch export. Pro never buys a more accurate number — the engine is the same engine on both tiers, and your export is never gated.
  • Purchase terms. CLEANPLATE Pro is $149.99 once for lifetime, or $59.99 a year. There is no free trial and no monthly plan; the free tier is the trial, and it is the whole instrument. The lifetime purchase is a single payment, never renews, and there is nothing to cancel. The yearly plan renews automatically at the then-current price for the same period unless renewal is turned off at least 24 hours before the period ends. Cancel it any time in your store account — on iOS, Settings › your name › Subscriptions; on Android, the Play Store › Payments and subscriptions. Cancelling stops the next charge and the period you have paid for runs to its end. We cannot cancel it for you, and the app will not ask you to stay.
  • My Pro plan lapsed. Nothing of yours is deleted, hidden or truncated. Every stand and every read stays readable and exportable, however many there are, and both exports come back byte-identical. What stops is starting a new stand beyond the first, saving a fourth read on it, replaying a read as target bands, drawing the drift, editing your own conventions and exporting in batch. A lapse lowers a ceiling on new work; it never takes a record away.
  • Prove it. Open the “Prove it” screen and CLEANPLATE re-runs ten bundled answer-key cases on your own handset and shows its answers beside the expected ones. The ten are chosen to be representative rather than flattering: seven the engine gets right, one it gets wrong, and two the capture gate refuses.
  • Does it need a signal on set? No. The whole measurement is arithmetic on the pixels of one frame, so the app opens, measures, prescribes and exports with the network switched off.
  • Do I need the printed targets? For the photometric figures — evenness, spill, purity, the exposure separation, the shadow fraction — you need the strip in the subject plane, because every value is read off it. The separation in metres additionally needs the square taped on the cloth and a 35 mm-equivalent focal length, which the app reads from the file’s EXIF where the camera wrote it and which you can set once in Settings otherwise. Every other figure is scale-free and needs no lens data at all.
  • Reset or delete my data. Your work is stored on your device. Clearing the app's data or uninstalling the app removes it. There is no server-side account to delete.
  • Report a bug or request a feature. Email us with what you expected and what happened — screenshots help a lot.

Sources & attribution

CLEANPLATE’s engine was written against an answer key and a null baseline both recorded before any solver code existed: a physically-based renderer with known lighting, known bounce and known geometry, checked against independent open-source keyers used only inside the harness. Across 120 cases it holds evenness within ±0.20 stop on 96.6% of regions (bias +0.028, RMS 0.184 stop) and the exposure separation within ±0.15 stop on 98.9%, against a null baseline of 0.0% and 8.9% at RMS 1.72 and 1.25 stops. Two thresholds are reported as named misses rather than passes: the single-frame spill index reaches 88.4% within ±0.05 against a 95% target, and rank correlation against a global-threshold reference keyer measures 0.496 against a 0.80 target. With a second frame taken with the backing lights off, the spill index reaches 95.1% within ±0.05 at RMS 0.029. The reference keyers used in the harness are FFmpeg (GPL-3.0), OpenCV (Apache-2.0) and PyMatting (MIT); none of them ships inside the app, and no code from any of them is distributed with it. Every figure above, the eight corrections made to the frozen renderer, the one engine constant calibrated against the key and the seven hypotheses tried and removed are recorded in the app’s own answer-key log.
