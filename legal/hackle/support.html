Common questions

  • Restore a purchase. Open the purchase screen in the app and choose "Restore Purchases". Purchases are tied to your app-store account and restore for free on your devices.
  • What is free and what is Pro. The whole solve is free forever: every trade name, every origin, use and destination, the quoted rule text with every citation and source date, the listing line and the struck names, one state row of your own, and CSV and JSON export. Free keeps the last 10 filed rows in the drawer. Pro adds the unlimited drawer, the re-solve that shows you which filed rows now read differently and why, a state row for every jurisdiction you sell into, and drawer search. Pro never buys a different verdict.
  • Subscription terms. Pro is $29.99 a year with a 21-day free trial (yearly plan only, offered once per customer), or $4.99 a month with no trial, or $79.99 once for lifetime. The yearly and monthly plans renew automatically at the then-current price for the same period unless renewal is turned off at least 24 hours before the period ends; the lifetime purchase is a single payment, never renews, and there is nothing to cancel. Cancel a renewing plan any time in your store account — on iOS, Settings › your name › Subscriptions; on Android, the Play Store › Payments and subscriptions. Cancelling stops the next charge and the period you have paid for runs to its end.
  • My Pro plan lapsed. Nothing of yours is deleted or hidden. Every filed row stays exactly where it is, stays readable, stays editable, stays deletable and stays exportable in full by both CSV and JSON, including rows filed past the free ten. A lapse lowers a ceiling on new records; it never takes a record away.
  • HACKLE is not legal advice and does not tell you what you may do. It reports what the federal provisions it quotes say about the taxon you named, for the use and destination you chose, and it shows you the text and the date it was read. It does not decide your case, does not speak for any agency, and does not address permits, tribal enrolment, religious use or eagle parts beyond quoting the statutory prohibition. The U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service and the law of the state you are selling into govern; the app does not.
  • Where the rules come from, and how stale they can be. The species membership tables and the quoted provisions come from the eCFR, Title 50, at a single stated issue date, plus two quoted sections of the U.S. Code. Every verdict on screen carries the citation and the date that copy was read, so you can always see how old it is. Rules change between our releases. Check the current provision before you publish a listing.
  • Why it sometimes refuses to name a bird. Many trade names cover more than one species. When a typed name maps to more than one taxon HACKLE returns the candidates and asks you to choose rather than guessing, because guessing the taxon would silently change the answer.
  • What re-solving does. A filed row records the source date and the key digest it was solved with. When you add or change a state row, or a newer table ships, HACKLE tells you which filed rows would now read differently and prints the difference before it rewrites anything.
  • Reset or delete my data. Your work is stored on your device. Clearing the app's data or uninstalling the app removes it. There is no server-side account to delete.
  • Report a bug or request a feature. Email us with what you expected and what happened — screenshots help a lot.

Sources & attribution

HACKLE’s species membership test, its provision selection and its trade-name resolver are implemented directly from the published texts they cite — 50 CFR 10.13 and 17.11 and fourteen provisions of 50 CFR parts 20, 21, 22 and 23, together with 16 U.S.C. 668(a) and 3372(a)(1) — all reproduced under 17 U.S.C. 105 and Georgia v. Public.Resource.Org, Inc., 590 U.S. 255 (2020). Scientific and common names for taxa the cited lists do not themselves name are taken from the Integrated Taxonomic Information System (ITIS), and every such name carries its authority on screen. The method and every constant are cited line by line in the app’s design record.
