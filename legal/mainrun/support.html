Common questions

  • Restore a purchase. Open the purchase screen in the app and choose "Restore Purchases". Purchases are tied to your app-store account and restore for free on your devices.
  • What is free and what is Pro. The instrument is free forever for a plain rectangular room: the centred origin solve in imperial and metric, the runner direction, the full setting-out sheet, the per-wall cut list, the take-off, unlimited saved rooms in the room book, every export and import, the cited rules and the “Prove it” screen. Pro adds the constrained solve — L-shaped, U-shaped and multi-wall rooms, columns and bulkheads, and light fittings landed on whole modules — the setting-out sheet for those rooms, and THE STANDING SPEC, a saved rule set that carries onto every later job. Pro never buys a different origin: the solver is the same solver on both tiers, and your own rooms are never gated.
  • Subscription terms. MAINRUN Pro is $59.99 a year and opens with a 21-day free introductory offer (annual plan only, offered once per store account), or $8.99 a month with no trial, or $179.99 once for lifetime. The yearly and monthly plans renew automatically at the then-current price for the same period unless renewal is turned off at least 24 hours before the period ends; the lifetime purchase is a single payment, never renews, and there is nothing to cancel. Cancel a renewing plan any time in your store account — on iOS, Settings › your name › Subscriptions; on Android, the Play Store › Payments and subscriptions. Cancelling stops the next charge and the period you have paid for runs to its end.
  • My Pro plan lapsed. Nothing of yours is deleted or hidden. Every room you entered on Pro still opens, still shows its plan, still prints its sheet and its cut list, still exports as JSON and still deletes on the free tier, including room books far longer than the free tier would let you build today. What stops is solving a new room that is not a plain rectangle, landing new fittings on whole modules, and the standing spec. A lapse lowers a ceiling on new work; it never takes a record away.
  • How the solve works. You give MAINRUN the room and the module — nothing else is required. It works in exact integer units (one unit is 1/2032 inch, so both 1/16 of an inch and one millimetre are represented exactly and nothing rounds twice), then searches the origin and the runner direction against a ranked objective: no border tile narrower than half a module, the two facing borders on each axis equal, the whole grid symmetrical about the room centre lines, and fixed fittings landing on whole modules. Every number opens to the arithmetic that produced it, and the setting-out sheet prints the two datum dimension chains a chalk line can actually be pulled from.
  • Where the rules come from. The default dimension rules are quoted from United States Government construction specifications that are in the public domain under 17 U.S.C. §105 — principally UFGS 09 51 00 §3.1.3 (“units less than one-half width are minimized”) and VA 09 51 00 §3.1.A (“symmetrically about center lines”). Each default prints its clause beside it and every one of them is editable per room. The governing authority for your job is whatever your drawings, your manufacturer and your inspector say, and MAINRUN shows that line on the plan, in Settings and in the sheet header.
  • Prove it. The app carries 23 worked cases hand-derived from those clauses. Open the “Prove it” screen and MAINRUN re-solves all of them on your own handset in about fifteen milliseconds and shows its answers against the known ones, so you can check the instrument rather than trust it.
  • What it will not do. MAINRUN sets out a grid. It is not a code-compliance check, it does not do fire-rated or seismic design, it carries no structural calculation and it does not certify anything. It will not tell you a ceiling is approved. It gives you an origin, a runner direction, a schedule, a take-off and a cut list, and it shows you the arithmetic behind all of them.
  • Imperial or metric — or both. The sheet prints in both systems side by side. Type dimensions the way a tradesman says them (13′6, 13′ 6″, 4115, 4115mm) and MAINRUN reads them.
  • Who this is for. The one-crew installer working the rooms that arrive without a reflected ceiling plan. If your job already comes with a drawn ceiling plan, MAINRUN has nothing to add to it.
  • Reset or delete my data. Your work is stored on your device. Clearing the app's data or uninstalling the app removes it. There is no server-side account to delete.
  • Report a bug or request a feature. Email us with what you expected and what happened — screenshots help a lot.

Sources & attribution

MAINRUN’s solver is measured on the device and in its build against an answer key with three ranked thresholds: 300 of 300 rooms in exact agreement with an independent exhaustive reference over 237,776,256 enumerated candidates; 0 invariant violations across 13 invariant families over 1,000 rooms; and 23 of 23 worked cases hand-derived from the cited clauses. A deliberately weak “divide and hope” baseline, recorded before the solver existed, fails the first and the third (0 of 300 and 9 of 23). Against that baseline over 1,000 rooms, sliver border tiles fall from 1,464 to 155, visibly unequal facing pairs from 2,324 to 981, and total border asymmetry from 731,441 to 99,685 marking steps; head to head MAINRUN is strictly better on 997 rooms, ties 3 and is worse on none. The rule text is taken from Unified Facilities Guide Specification 09 51 00 and Department of Veterans Affairs specification 09 51 00, both works of the United States Government and in the public domain under 17 U.S.C. §105. The method, every threshold, every constant and the price the objective pays in extra cut tiles are cited line by line in the app’s design record and its answer key.
