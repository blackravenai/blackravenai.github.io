Common questions

  • Restore a purchase. Open the purchase screen in the app and choose "Restore Purchases". Purchases are tied to your app-store account and restore for free on your devices.
  • What is free and what is Pro. The instrument is free forever, at the same accuracy Pro gets: one plan on the shelf checked in full — all eleven reconciliation families, the complete arithmetic printed beside every line that disagrees, the setting-out rod, your rulings remembered, the latest check and its diff against the check before it, the findings CSV, a complete JSON backup, the worked example and the “Prove it” self-test. Pro adds every plan you sell rather than one, the whole dated check history with the diff against any earlier version, saved house-rule profiles, and the Markdown release report. Pro never buys a more accurate answer — the eleven families are the same eleven families on both tiers, and export is never gated.
  • Subscription terms. SCRIBE Pro is $34.99 a year and opens with a 21-day free introductory offer (annual plan only, offered once per store account), or $5.99 a month with no trial, or $89.99 once for lifetime. The yearly and monthly plans renew automatically at the then-current price for the same period unless renewal is turned off at least 24 hours before the period ends; the lifetime purchase is a single payment, never renews, and there is nothing to cancel. Cancel a renewing plan any time in your store account — on iOS, Settings › your name › Subscriptions; on Android, the Play Store › Payments and subscriptions. Cancelling stops the next charge and the period you have paid for runs to its end.
  • My Pro plan lapsed. Nothing of yours is deleted or hidden. Every plan you filed on Pro still opens, still checks, still shows its findings and their arithmetic, still exports as CSV and JSON, and still deletes on the free tier — including shelves far longer than the free tier would let you build today. Check history beyond the free window is withheld from the table rather than deleted, and the app says so in those words and still includes it in your export. What stops is adding a new plan beyond the first, reading the whole history, saving house-rule profiles and printing the release report. A lapse lowers a ceiling on new work; it never takes a record away.
  • How the check works. You paste your plan in as you already wrote it — the cut list, the part schedule, the materials or shopping list, and the numbered steps — or you paste the CSV that OpenCutList or a Fusion 360 cut-list export already produces. SCRIBE parses the four sections, binds every part letter and every named material back to the line that declares it, and runs eleven families over that binding: a size that disagrees between two places, a material that disagrees, a quantity that disagrees, a part the steps use that the cut list never cuts, a part cut but never used, a step pointing at a part or a step number that does not exist, hardware demanded in more places than the list holds, stock that will not yield the parts it is supposed to yield, an assembly whose parts do not close to the stated overall dimension, a number written so it cannot be read, and one letter used for two different parts. Every finding opens to the two figures, the operation between them and the line numbers they came from, computed in exact fractions — never in floating point, so 1/3 of 5-1/4 is not 1.7499999.
  • Prove it. The app carries synthetic plans whose faults are declared by hand, not read back out of the engine. Open the “Prove it” screen and SCRIBE re-runs them on your own handset and shows its answers against the declared ones, so you can check the instrument rather than trust it.
  • What it will not do, and this matters. SCRIBE finds disagreement, not wrongness. If a dimension is wrong in the same way everywhere it appears, nothing disagrees and SCRIBE will not see it. It does not read drawings, photographs or SketchUp models, it does not judge whether a joint is sound, and it makes no structural or safety claim of any kind. Finding nothing is not a guarantee that the plan is correct, and the app says so in those words on its own “How it works” screen.
  • Lines it cannot read are reported, not skipped. A line SCRIBE cannot parse is raised by line number as unreadable rather than passed over in silence, so the coverage figure means what it says and a section of loose prose can never be mistaken for a section that closed.
  • Reset or delete my data. Your work is stored on your device. Clearing the app's data or uninstalling the app removes it. There is no server-side account to delete.
  • Report a bug or request a feature. Email us with what you expected and what happened — screenshots help a lot.
