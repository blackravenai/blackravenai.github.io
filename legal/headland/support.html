Common questions

  • Restore a purchase. Open the purchase screen in the app and choose "Restore Purchases". Purchases are tied to your app-store account and restore for free on your devices.
  • What HEADLAND computes, and what it never decides. HEADLAND is a setting-out instrument. It resolves geometry on the numbers you enter: where each row stake goes on the baseline, where every line post stands so that none of them lands on a vine, how many vines each row carries, how much wire each run needs and where it splices. It computes no structural or agronomic result of any kind — no anchor holding capacity, no post embedment, no wind or crop loading, no variety, rootstock, spacing or training recommendation. Every dimension in a plan is your own entry or a dated, cited default you can change. Your site, your agronomist and your supplier’s instructions govern, and the app says so on its own surface.
  • Where the default numbers come from. The starting spacings and the reference figures are transcribed from Iowa State University Extension’s published vineyard establishment material (Domoto et al., 2002) and are cited in the app. They are defaults, dated and editable, not recommendations — change them to whatever your block actually uses.
  • What the solver searches, and the one thing it will not do. HEADLAND searches even post runs: one bay length held down the whole row, so a crew can hold one tape number. Where no even run fits between your post-spacing limits with the clearance you set beside a vine, it says so and names the constraint that bound it, and offers a labelled staggered plan rather than printing a number that cannot be driven. On about 3.6% of rows a staggered bay would use one post fewer than the best even run; the app detects and states every one of those rows on the row itself, so the extra post is your decision and never a silent charge.
  • Rows that cannot be fitted. When a row has no legal plan, HEADLAND declines it in flagging orange and names the binding constraint, then offers the smallest single change to your own entry — the post band, or the clearance beside a vine — that would make it fit. It states arithmetic on your numbers; it never recommends. Applying an offer is one tap and undoable.
  • What is free and what is Pro. Free forever, with no time limit and no read-only mode: one saved block solved at full accuracy — the same solver, nothing held back — the plate, every row stake and every post station on the per-row chainage sheet, the plant schedule, the wire runs and the splice points including multi-wire trellis, the totals rail, the “Prove it” screen, and CSV and JSON export always. Pro adds every block of the season kept in the block book, start-from on any prior block’s spec, the as-driven records, the season tally and the itemised take-off order sheet. Pro never buys a more accurate number — a free user and a Pro user solving the same block get identical plans — and your own data is never gated.
  • Subscription terms. HEADLAND Pro is $39.99 a year and opens with a 21-day free introductory offer (annual plan only, offered once per store account), or $7.99 a month with no trial, or $129.99 once for lifetime. The yearly and monthly plans renew automatically at the then-current price for the same period unless renewal is turned off at least 24 hours before the period ends; the lifetime purchase is a single payment, never renews, and there is nothing to cancel. Cancel a renewing plan any time in your store account — on iOS, Settings › your name › Subscriptions; on Android, the Play Store › Payments and subscriptions. Cancelling stops the next charge and the period you have paid for runs to its end. We cannot cancel it for you, and the app will not ask you to stay.
  • My Pro plan lapsed. Nothing of yours is deleted, hidden or truncated. Every block in the book, every as-driven record and both exports stay readable, exportable and deletable, however many there are. What stops is starting a new block beyond the first, recording new as-driven posts, and opening the take-off and the season tally. A lapse lowers a ceiling on new work; it never takes a record away.
  • Prove it. Open the “Prove it” screen and HEADLAND re-solves a set of rows on your own handset against an exhaustive search computed in front of you, and shows its answers beside the expected ones — so you can check the instrument rather than trust it.
  • Does it need a signal in the field? No. The whole solve is arithmetic in the app, so it opens, solves, draws the plate and exports with the phone in airplane mode, standing on the baseline of a block with no bars.
  • Reset or delete my data. Your work is stored on your device. Clearing the app's data or uninstalling the app removes it. There is no server-side account to delete.
  • Report a bug or request a feature. Email us with what you expected and what happened — screenshots help a lot.

Sources & attribution

HEADLAND’s reference defaults are measurements and bare numeric rules transcribed from Iowa State University Extension’s published vineyard establishment material (Domoto et al., 2002); facts and measurements, not expression, and cited in the app. No terms notice appears on that source and no public-domain status is claimed for it. The engine was written against an answer key recorded before any solver code existed: it agrees with an exhaustive enumeration of legal post plans on 6,904 of 6,904 cases, commits zero invariant violations across 881,913 row segments, reproduces 55 of 55 transcribed published numbers, and detects 163 of 163 rows where the even-run restriction costs a post and states each one on the row. A null baseline — the per-acre spreadsheet rule the field uses today — agrees on 532 of the same 6,904 cases, puts 3,066,181 posts on a vine and prints a plan for 2,352 rows that have none.
