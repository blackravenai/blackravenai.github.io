Common questions

  • Restore a purchase. Open the purchase screen in the app and choose "Restore Purchases". Purchases are tied to your app-store account and restore for free on your devices.
  • What WATERTABLE does, and does not do. It plans course layout and nothing else: it tells you the exposure to hang at and hands you the marks for the stick. It gives no weather-resistance, flashing, fastening, wind-load or structural output and makes no code claim. Every exposure limit is your own entry, taken from the manufacturer’s guidance you cite; the default rule the app explains is USDA Agriculture Handbook No. 73, a public-domain United States Government publication. Check the pole against the wall before the first course is nailed.
  • What is free and what is Pro. One wall of a house is free forever and nothing about it is cut down: the exposure searched across your whole band, the three-valued verdict on every opening, the complete story pole sheet in feet, inches and sixteenths with the millimetre beside it, the Prove-it screen, three houses in the book, and CSV and JSON export of everything you typed. Pro adds every wall of the house with the scheme carried round the corners, graduated runs, the book past three houses, and the landing record. Pro never buys a better plan — the solver is identical on both tiers, and your own data is never gated.
  • Subscription terms. WATERTABLE Pro is $49.99 a year and opens with a 21-day free trial (yearly plan only, offered once per store account), or $8.99 a month with no trial, or $149.99 once for lifetime. The yearly and monthly plans renew automatically at the then-current price for the same period unless renewal is turned off at least 24 hours before the period ends; the lifetime purchase is a single payment, never renews, and there is nothing to cancel. Cancel a renewing plan any time in your store account — on iOS, Settings › your name › Subscriptions; on Android, the Play Store › Payments and subscriptions. Cancelling stops the next charge and the period you have paid for runs to its end.
  • My Pro plan lapsed. Nothing of yours is deleted or hidden. Every house in the book stays readable, stays editable, still solves its first wall, still prints its sheet and still exports as CSV and JSON — including houses with more walls than the free tier would let you create today. What stops is adding a wall to a new house, graduated mode, a fourth house and the landing record. A lapse lowers a ceiling on new work; it never takes a record away.
  • It says no plan closes inside my cap. That is the instrument refusing rather than guessing. At a tight deviation cap there is often no whole number of courses that reaches a head height inside the band you entered, and WATERTABLE says so, names the binding constraint, and tells you the cap at which it does close — so you can loosen the cap or accept the notch before anything is cut, instead of finding out at the window.
  • The corner rule in graduated mode. A wall whose courses change exposure as they rise cannot carry that scheme unchanged round a corner. In graduated mode WATERTABLE reports the corner result instead of enforcing it, and says so on the screen where the plan is drawn.
  • Prove it. The app carries public-domain test cases from USDA Agriculture Handbook No. 73 and its own migration checks. Open the Prove-it screen and WATERTABLE re-solves them on your handset, in front of you, and shows its answer against the known one — so you can check the instrument rather than trust it.
  • Feet, inches and sixteenths. Every figure is entered and printed the way the trade reads it, with the millimetre alongside. The app accepts the dialects a carpenter actually types — 8′ 1 3/16″, 97.1875, 97 3/16 and the rest.
  • Measure the wall yourself. WATERTABLE plans from the figures you type. It does not measure your house, check your water table or verify your product’s exposure band. A head height typed wrong will produce a pole marked wrong.
  • Reset or delete my data. Your work is stored on your device. Clearing the app's data or uninstalling the app removes it. There is no server-side account to delete.
  • Report a bug or request a feature. Email us with what you expected and what happened — screenshots help a lot.
